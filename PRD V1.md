Product Requirements Document (PRD)

Product Name

Parent Operating System (POS) — Montessori Home Execution System for Ages 2–6

Document Version

v1.0

Purpose

POS is a parent-friendly system that helps families execute a high-quality Montessori-aligned home routine for children ages 2–6, with clear activity mapping, year/month/week planning, weekend tracking, status management, gotchas, and reminders for what must not be missed.

The product is designed to make parents confident, consistent, and effective without requiring them to be Montessori experts.


---

1) Problem Statement

Parents often know what outcomes they want for their child—curiosity, independence, problem-solving, communication, discipline, and arts—but they lack a simple operating system to:

know what to do each day,

track progress meaningfully,

avoid common mistakes,

stay consistent over months and years,

and turn Montessori principles into practical household execution.


As a result, good intentions often become ad hoc routines, over-helping, too much screen time, worksheet overload, inconsistent practice, or anxiety-driven parenting.


---

2) Product Goal

Create a home-based parent system that:

1. maps activities to child development goals,


2. provides yearly, monthly, weekly, and weekend tracking,


3. supports status tracking for each activity and review cycle,


4. highlights gotchas and important-not-to-miss items,


5. keeps parents consistent and confident,


6. makes teaching at home simple and repeatable.

12) Status Tracking Requirements

Every activity, week, and month must support the following statuses:

Pending — not started

In Progress — started but not yet complete

Completed — achieved for the intended period


Optional status flags:

Blocked

Skipped intentionally

Needs review

Repeated due to regression


Status Rules

A weekly item can only be marked completed when all core activities are either completed or intentionally skipped with a note.

A monthly item can only be completed after a weekend review is recorded for at least 3 weeks in the month.

A yearly goal can only be completed when the month-level milestones are met with evidence.

2. Activity status summary


3. Wins


4. Struggles

7. Parent notes


8. Adjustment plan




---

14) Weekend Review Output Format

Each weekend review should generate a simple summary:

In Progress: items started but not yet stable

Pending: items not started or missed

Completed: items achieved with observable evidence


Example:

In Progress: pouring without spilling

Pending: storytelling with full sentences

Completed: spoon transfer activity

Gotcha Behavior

---

17) Important Not to Miss

A mandatory highlight block that appears at the top of every week and month.

Important Not to Miss Examples

15–20 minutes of uninterrupted concentration daily

One read-aloud session daily

One real-world practical task per week

One open-ended problem per week

One parent-child narration session every weekend

One arts activity per week with no template

One observation note before giving help


The product should visually emphasize these items so parents do not skip them.

27) Risks and Mitigations

Risk: Too much tracking becomes stressful

Mitigation: low-friction summaries and optional detail depth

Risk: Parents over-optimize and lose joy

Mitigation: include play, rest, and flexible modes

Risk: Too many activities cause fatigue

Mitigation: daily minimal plan + weekly core plan

Risk: Parents confuse completion with learning

Mitigation: show observation and behavior change as the main success markers


---

28) Definition of Done for a Week

A week is considered successful when:

core activities are completed or intentionally adjusted,

weekend review is filled,

at least one gotcha is checked,

at least one important-not-to-miss item is addressed,

a parent note is recorded,

the next week is prepared.



---

29) Definition of Done for a Month

A month is complete when:

all weekly reviews are recorded,

monthly theme objectives are met or adjusted,

skill trends are visible,

recurring gotchas are handled,

the next month plan is ready.



---

30) Definition of Done for a Year

A year is complete when:

age-band outcomes are reviewed,

the child’s skill profile is updated,

top strengths and gaps are captured,

next-year recommendations are set,

transition readiness is documented.



---

31) Final Product Vision

POS should become the parent’s trusted operating system for home learning: simple enough to use every week, rigorous enough to matter, and friendly enough that grandparents, caregivers, and busy parents can all use it.

The system should help a child grow into someone who is curious, thoughtful, articulate, resilient, and creative — without turning childhood into a worksheet factory.
